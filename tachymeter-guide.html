Guide II

Using a Tachymeter

The numbers around a chronograph’s bezel turn elapsed time into speed. Once you know the trick, it takes seconds.

Sections
05
Reading
5 min
Level
All
Section 01 / 05

What the scale shows

A tachymeter scale shows units per hour. Time something over one unit of distance, and the number under the seconds hand is its speed in units per hour.

Section 02 / 05

The basic method

  1. Start the chronograph as you pass a marker, such as a kilometre post.
  2. Stop it at the next marker.
  3. Read the tachymeter number beside the seconds hand: that is your speed in km/h.

36 seconds over a kilometre reads 100: you were travelling at 100 km/h.

Section 03 / 05

The formula behind it

The scale is simply 3600 divided by the elapsed seconds. That is why it starts around 7 seconds (about 500) and ends at 60 seconds (60): beyond a minute, the scale no longer applies.

Section 04 / 05

Beyond speed

The scale measures any rate per hour. Time how long it takes to make one item, and the tachymeter tells you how many you could make in an hour. It works for laps, pages or parcels.

Section 05 / 05

Telemeter and pulsometer

Some dials carry other scales. A telemeter estimates distance from the delay between seeing lightning and hearing thunder. A pulsometer counts a set number of heartbeats and reads the beats per minute.
