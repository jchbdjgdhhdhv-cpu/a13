Guide I

How a Chronograph Works

A chronograph adds a stopwatch to a watch. Here is what each pusher, hand and dial does, and what to look for inside.

Sections
05
Reading
5 min
Level
All
Section 01 / 05

Two pushers, one sequence

The pusher above the crown starts and stops the chronograph; the one below resets it. The sequence is always start, stop, reset. Pressing reset while the chronograph runs can damage some movements, so stop first.

Section 02 / 05

The central seconds hand

On most chronographs the long central hand is not the time-of-day seconds: it stays at zero until you start the chronograph. The running seconds of the time usually live in a small subdial instead.

Section 03 / 05

Reading the subdials

  • Minute counter: counts elapsed minutes, often to 30.
  • Hour counter: counts elapsed hours, often to 12.
  • Running seconds: the time-of-day seconds, always moving.

Add the readings together: 2 hours, 14 minutes and 32 seconds on the central hand.

Section 04 / 05

Column wheel or cam

Inside, the chronograph is controlled either by a column wheel, a small castellated wheel prized for its smooth pusher feel, or by a cam, which is simpler and robust. Both are reliable; the column wheel is the traditional choice in finer movements.

Section 05 / 05

Flyback and split-seconds

A flyback lets you reset and restart with a single press, useful for timing back-to-back intervals. A split-seconds (rattrapante) has two central hands, so you can stop one for an intermediate time while the other keeps running.
